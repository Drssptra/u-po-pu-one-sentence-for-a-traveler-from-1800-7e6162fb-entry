One sentence, 176 characters, that explains the modern internet to a time traveler who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — using only things such a traveler could already picture: every library, post office and market stall in the world joined by one road, so that a letter written in London reaches a stranger in Boston before a candle burns down. No word in it was coined after the traveler's time; the road is the single comparison that carries the whole picture, and the letter arriving in moments is what the internet actually does.
